I made my first open-source contribution, and my fix got merged!

Surprisingly, the hardest part wasn't writing the code. It was finding a meaningful issue that matched my skills and interests.

I explored hundreds of GitHub issues, evaluating their relevance, complexity, and feasibility. Along the way, I built reusable AI-assisted workflows for discovering, evaluating, and testing issues, improving the consistency of my work.

Eventually, I contributed to RepoDx, a Python tool that detects exposed secrets in code repositories. I identified why it was missing Linear API keys, implemented a fix, and added regression tests. My contribution was incorporated into a maintainer's pull request and merged upstream!

This experience also made me think about the potential of open source in the age of AI. In many ways, open-source contribution is a form of pooling our computing resources, skills, and creativity for the greater good. Individually, we may not have access to enormous computing power, but through small contributions, amplified by AI and shared openly, we can collectively build incredible software.

A small but exciting first step!
